GDS v.4 — a dynamic gravitational field

GDS (Gravitational Dynamical System) is a research framework describing gravitational interaction, the evolution of matter and cosmological evolution through a single nonlinear dynamical field. GDS v.4 builds on the original GDS v.3 and develops its physical and mathematical formulation alongside insights into nonlinear stability from GSC-X research.

Within its own development programme, the framework has undergone an in-depth gravitational audit addressing physical interpretation, consistency of the equations, numerical implementation and continuity with previous results. Version 4 development continues with the perturbation system, gravitational response and conservation laws.

GDS is being developed as an extension of the gravitational description of General Relativity (GR), incorporating variable field dynamics dependent on time and space while retaining continuity with its verified limits. The cosmological branch also develops and compares the phenomenology of the standard ΛCDM model: cosmic expansion, structure growth, the cosmic microwave background, gravitational lensing and baryon acoustic oscillations.

The central idea is to interpret effects attributed to dark matter and dark energy as different regimes of the same gravitationally dynamic field rather than as two separate physical entities. At galactic scales, the research examines gravitational response to baryonic matter and the emergence of effective halo structures. Cosmological studies investigate time- and scale-dependent effective gravity and its influence on the early and late Universe.

Another part of the research addresses gravitational collapse beyond the event horizon, dynamic saturation and the formation of a finite core instead of a classical singularity. Related work explores possible signatures in gravitational-wave ringdown, energy transfer and cosmogenesis hypotheses. These directions form an evolving physical programme, with mathematical and numerical foundations for its individual mechanisms.

Regenerative metastability

A shared research theme of GDS and GSC-X is the long-term persistence of systems exposed to instability. Regenerative metastability describes a system’s ability to recognise, constrain, reorganise and repeatedly manage instability while maintaining a viable operating corridor.

Research into metastability corridors connects physical modelling with predictive stabilisation of engineered and other nonlinear systems. It investigates the interplay of stress, transition, recovery and persistence, and whether this organisational pattern can be described through a common architecture.

GSC-X Executive Kernel

GSC-X is a platform-independent predictive executive stabilization architecture designed for real-time monitoring, early-warning evaluation, decision support and adaptive stabilization of complex dynamic systems.

It can operate either as a standalone executive stabilization system or as an additional executive layer integrated above existing control, monitoring, automation and decision-support infrastructures.

The purpose of GSC-X is not to replace existing systems by default, but to provide an additional layer of predictive awareness, resilience evaluation and executive stabilization.

We consider the reproducibility of shared stabilization principles across different technological domains a distinctive contribution of the project. Documented validation campaigns achieved an unusual 100% success rate in the measured metric in selected configurations, such as system survival throughout the test. These results highlight the potential of a common architecture and always relate to specific conditions, scenarios and system versions.

Core Architecture

The current architecture is organized around the Executive Kernel: a unified processing pipeline for telemetry ingestion, dynamic state evaluation, transition analysis, decision support and validation feedback.

  • CM — Context Memory
  • ELI — Early Limit Indicator
  • PTM — Predictive Transition Memory
  • MR — Metastability Reserve
  • MRI — Metastability Risk Index
  • Executive State Engine
  • Executive Decision Layer

Together, these components allow GSC-X to evaluate whether a system is stable, degrading, recovering, approaching a critical transition or reorganizing within a metastable operating corridor.

Integration Model

GSC-X is designed to be platform-independent and interface-agnostic.

  • Linux, Windows, embedded and edge environments
  • Cloud and local runtime systems
  • Industrial controllers and automation platforms
  • CSV telemetry, REST APIs, OPC-UA, Modbus and custom telemetry interfaces
  • ROS / robotics pipelines
  • Digital Twins and replay validation environments

No vendor lock-in. No mandatory infrastructure replacement.

Proprietary Implementation

GSC-X is proprietary research software. Public repositories provide validation artifacts, benchmark results, documentation and reproducibility materials, while the complete proprietary Executive Kernel implementation is not publicly distributed.

Research Direction

Current research includes the continued development of the Executive Kernel and an evolving Universal Metastability Kernel undergoing cross-domain validation across independent engineering and scientific environments.

This research investigates how complex systems can maintain long-term stability, resilience and operational persistence through predictive state evaluation, transition awareness and adaptive executive stabilization.

Our next objective is to reproduce these principles in further independent systems and test whether similarly high success rates can be repeated beyond the environments studied so far. Planned directions include autonomous driving, predictive security for banking and payment systems, anomaly detection and other critical infrastructure. These are future research directions for which we will develop dedicated scenarios, metrics and reproducible tests.

Vision

The long-term vision of GSC-X is to provide a common executive stabilization architecture for complex technological, industrial, scientific and infrastructure systems.

Its goal is to help organizations detect instability earlier, reduce operational risk, improve resilience, support better decisions and maintain stability under changing or uncertain conditions.

Rather than replacing existing technologies, GSC-X is designed to enhance them with predictive executive intelligence, helping organizations improve stability, resilience and long-term operational performance.

NEXUSReality analysis

We are currently developing our most ambitious project — NEXUS. Its goal is to cross-analyse data from telescopes and observatories and identify connections that may remain hidden when individual observations are processed in isolation.

NEXUS is intended to compare observations from independent sources, assess how theories and hypotheses agree with the data, and evaluate which interpretations the available measurements support and where discrepancies arise. The ambition is not only to examine existing explanations, but also to uncover new patterns, formulate new research questions and potentially discover further insights into physical reality.
