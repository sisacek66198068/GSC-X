NEXUS V1 DEVELOPMENT · REALITY ANALYSIS

A cross-observatory architecture for testing physical theories,
hypotheses and exploring new principles of reality.

Our most ambitious project

NEXUS is our most ambitious research and development project, currently progressing towards V1 completion: a cross-observatory analytical architecture intended to connect measurements from independent ground-based and space instruments. The goal is to examine the same objects and phenomena from multiple perspectives, compare physical explanations and uncover connections that may remain hidden in isolated analyses.

The long-term aim is to work with every scientifically useful type of data and measurement available from existing telescopes, observatories and their historical archives. Coverage will grow progressively according to data availability, permissions, calibrations and the evaluation of individual integration layers.

From diverse measurements to shared context

Planned inputs include images and maps, spectra and spectral cubes, light curves, pulsar timing, astrometry, redshifts, polarimetry, interferometric measurements, catalogues and individual detection lists. Further directions include cosmological maps, gravitational waves, neutrino events and related experimental products.

Each measurement type requires its own interpretation. NEXUS is intended to align object identification, coordinates, time references, units and uncertainty information while preserving instrument response, calibrations and data provenance. This is essential for meaningful comparisons between observatories.

A cross-evaluation architecture

The proposed architecture connects data ingestion and quality assessment, object and event matching, dynamic-state analysis using GSC-X and comparison of physical-model predictions with observations. Its ambition is unusually broad integration of instruments, data modalities and theories within a reproducible analytical workflow.

Outputs are intended to provide traceable assessments: which measurements support an interpretation, where discrepancies occur, how uncertainties affect the result and what further observation could distinguish competing hypotheses. NEXUS is intended to compare standard models, GDS and other alternatives against the same criteria.

New physics as a testable possibility

NEXUS is intended to identify candidate anomalies, recurring dynamic patterns and discrepancies between models and measurements. These could motivate new hypotheses concerning gravity, stellar and plasma dynamics, compact objects or cosmic evolution.

A candidate new physical phenomenon must withstand alternative explanations, systematic errors and independent data. We therefore plan separate test datasets, checks for instrumental effects and reproducible model comparisons. The objective is to generate and evaluate testable insights rather than automatically treating a discrepancy as a discovery.

Current status as of 18 November 2026

The internal technical estimate for NEXUS V1 is 82–85% complete (around 84%). It reflects documented capabilities, completed validation and the target architecture. The individual estimates describe progress as of this update.

The last completed autonomous research checkpoint is Cycle 9061. Cycle 9062 was interrupted during a planned operational pause and did not require a scientific or technical decision by a human.

Autonomous research engine · approximately 95%

The supervisor runs long series of experiments without manual instructions for each step. The research layer, supported by AI Codex, reads the research charter, past results, failure archive and system state. It selects an informative next step, builds and tests analytical procedures, challenges its findings and saves auditable checkpoints, including failed tests.

NEXUS labels results by origin as MEASURED / DERIVED / INFERRED / SIMULATED / UNKNOWN. It uses exact-byte hashes, reproducible replays, null tests, control scenarios and deliberate input mutations. Validation stops when required checks cannot be completed.

Testing GSC-X and GDS

GSC-X is used where there is a physical or statistical rationale. Prospective benchmarks compare it with boxcar, maximum-absolute and CUSUM methods. In one harder benchmark, GSC-X was less sensitive than boxcar. In other stress tests it controlled false alarms, while its response to very weak step signals remained small.

GSC-X scoring was transferred to real TESS data as a diagnostic. This establishes that the transfer was performed, while assessment of astronomical performance requires further tests. GSC-X Symbiont v.1 remains a separate test candidate awaiting its full comparative benchmark in NEXUS.

GDS is evaluated as a testable physical framework when a suitable observable and sufficiently informative data can distinguish competing hypotheses. NEXUS records when an experiment does not establish GDS relevance.

Real astronomical data

Development uses products from JWST/MIRI, JWST/NIRSpec and TESS as well as other archives and catalogues. Tests cover spectroscopy, relative astrometry, time series, periodic variability, detector structures and instrumental checks. They also examine FITS structure, units, time and coordinate systems, uncertainty, covariance, product identity and raw-data ancestry.

Outputs from the Astronomy and Sky branches are part of the research corpus and feed replay, provenance checks and decisions about admissible evidence.

Evidence independence and cross-observatory fusion · 75–80%

Before treating a result as physical evidence, NEXUS checks documented provenance and custody, target identity, time and coordinate references, calibration response, covariance, source exposures and reduction independence. Identical bytes, shared raw data or alternative processing of the same exposure do not by themselves constitute independent evidence.

Universal Acquisition Layer · 65–75%

Recognition of locally available products, hash identity, structure, measurement type and metadata has advanced. The target workflow is discover → acquire → verify provenance → interpret the product → assign an adapter → analyse.

The main bottleneck is independent observations with complete calibration, uncertainty information and provenance. A valuable next step would be an independently documented non-JWST X-ray product or a second genuinely independent reduction of an observation already analysed.

Scientific Adjudicator · 45–55%

This layer combines evidence admission rules, lineage tracking, hypothesis testing and decision checkpoints. Individual decisions can already be limited or rejected based on evidence quality. The unified layer is intended to document the basis, scope and limitations of each conclusion.

  • CONTRADICTED WITHIN TESTED DOMAIN
  • INSUFFICIENT DATA

Remaining integration work

New instruments and products may require a validated adapter and checks on units, coordinates, time references, calibration, instrument response, covariance and uncertainty. Fully autonomous acquisition and physical integration are still in development, especially for radio interferometry, gravitational waves, neutrinos, polarimetry and other specialised sources.

The unified conversational and graphical interface is internally estimated at 30–35%. Its goal is to launch a research mission from a question asked in ordinary language.

What a completed NEXUS V1 will mean

NEXUS is intended to turn a physics question in ordinary language into an auditable scientific mission. It will identify the target, locate suitable observations and verify their provenance, select analytical procedures, align time and spatial context, and assess uncertainties and systematic errors. It will distinguish dependent and independent evidence and compare competing hypotheses using conventional methods and, where appropriate, GSC-X and GDS.

The report will distinguish measurements, derived results, inferences and simulations. It will identify supporting and conflicting evidence, limitations, the degree of independence and further measurements that could distinguish competing explanations.
